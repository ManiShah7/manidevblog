# What I have learned as a web developer in my first year

I have been a professional web developer for about 1.5 years now. What do I mean by professional? Well, I mean I have been doing it as a job and earning money from it. I started learning web development (HTML, CSS and WordPress) back in 2016, and I finally managed to break into the industry in 2019. 

Before I did web development professionally, I had some ideas about how it would be like, but things were not exactly how I was expecting. I am going to share my experiences in the last 1.5 years as a professional web developer.

## Getting a job is important, so are money and mental health

> I just wanted to get a web development job and finally be happy. 

I went to a couple of job interviews before my first job. I was happy with even the lowest salary and didn't really care whether I liked that company or not. I just wanted to get a web development job and finally be happy. 

This motivated me to practice and learn programming every single day but I was so desperate to find a job that I didn't really negotiate my salary in my first job. Getting a job is great but if you are not getting paid enough, you will be really demotivated after a while. 

In addition to money, try to do some research about the companies that you apply for and see whether you like them or not. I am so happy that I wasn't successful in my first job interview because they had many negative reviews on Glassdoor but I still applied for it. My suggestion to you is DON'T! If you know you are going to hate working at a company, don't apply for a job in there. Be patient, keep learning and you will definitely find a better opportunity. 


## Your colleagues can be very smart developers, but a**hole human beings

> What is important is that you have a job and this is a chance for you to learn.

This is not true about every experienced web developer out there but if you are a junior web developer, some of your more senior colleagues might look down on you. 

They might hate their job or have some personal issues or even be natural jerks! That's not important. What is important is that you have a job and this is a chance for you to learn. 

Therefore, don't lose your hopes and even if you get offended, don't leave your job! Be smart. Get some experience, build your skills and leave. 

## You should be always learning but don't burn yourself out!

Programming is all about learning and it doesn't matter how much experience you have. I really enjoy learning new things but sometimes I burn myself out by spending lots of time on learning. For exmaple, I come home after a long day at work and try to work on my personal projects. My brain is litterally fried and can't process anything! Don't do this to yourself. 

Take a break, do something else and try to enjoy other things too (I know it's hard!).

## Don't hate or love programming languages or tools

I always thought I would use React or Node in my professional job but I'm a WordPress web developer now (No, I don't use Elementor or Divi, I develop custom themes!). I thought PHP was dead and WordPress is useless but these are not true. Even jQuery is not dead yet. 

When I started my first job, I found out how powerful (and even great) PHP is and how much money people can make from WordPress. As long as there are people out there who use something, there will be demand for it. If you use a language that you don't like at work, try to learn it. Exactly like how you are learning all those cool and modern things like React or Vue.

Don't limit yourself by thinking this language or that framework is dead. Your first job in this industry can be anything but you just need it on your resume. Once you have something on your resume, then you can choose what programming languages you want to use in your next jobs.
